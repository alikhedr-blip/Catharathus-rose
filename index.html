Comprehensive Research on the Vinca Plant (Catharanthus roseus)

Common Names: Periwinkle, pink periwinkle, rose periwinkle, Madagascar periwinkle[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span).

Latin (Botanic) Name: Catharanthus roseus (L.)[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span)

Family: Apocynaceae (Dogbane family)[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span)

1. History & Active Constituents

The natural Vinca plant ("Flowering Vinca") is considered a major treasure in the fields of pharmacy and pharmacognosy[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span). Here are comprehensive details regarding the substances found in the Vinca plant and their structural composition[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span).

The Vinca plant contains over 130 indole alkaloids[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span). The most medically significant of these—from which pharmaceutical agents are derived—include[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span):

  1. Vincristine (VCR)
    • Structure: A dimeric indole alkaloid composed of two units: catharanthine and vindoline[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span).
    • Pharmaceutical Significance: Plays a big role in treating childhood leukemias[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span). It is a revolutionary substance that increased the survival rate for childhood leukemia from less than 10% in 1960 to 90% today[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span).
  2. Vinblastine (VLB)
    • Structure: Structurally very similar to vincristine, differing only in a chemical group (a methyl group instead of a formyl group on the vindoline ring); however, this slight difference results in distinct pharmacokinetic properties and activity profiles[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span).
    • Activity: Inhibits cancer cell division; used in the treatment of breast, lung, ovarian, and testicular cancers[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span).
  3. Semi-synthetic Derivatives
    • Due to the low natural yield of these alkaloids from the plant, the pharmaceutical industry has developed semi-synthetic derivatives such as Vinorelbine and Vindesine to improve efficacy and reduce toxicity[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span).

2. Pharmacodynamics, History & Nomenclature

Overview and Reasons for Renown

  • Scientific Name: Catharanthus roseus (known as Madagascar Periwinkle)[span_15](start_span)[span_15](end_span).
  • Medical Significance: It revolutionized cancer treatment by yielding two compounds that rank among the earliest and most potent anti-cancer agents[span_16](start_span)[span_16](end_span).

Ethnobotanical History

  • Native Origin (Madagascar): Malagasy people including fishermen chewed its leaves to suppress hunger and fatigue; it was also used in traditional medicine[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span).
  • Historical Spread: The plant was dispersed by seafarers across the Indian Ocean during the era of Swahili trade (800–1450 AD)[span_18](start_span)[span_18](end_span).

Introduction to the West and Nomenclature

  • First European Record (1658): Étienne de Flacourt, governor of a French colony in Madagascar, documented the plant and sent its seeds to France[span_19](start_span)[span_19](end_span).
  • Chelsea Physic Garden: Philip Miller was the first to cultivate the plant and publish illustrations of it in Britain, which facilitated its spread throughout Europe[span_20](start_span)[span_20](end_span).

Modern Medical Discovery and Scientific Serendipity

  • Beginning of Research (1920s): Two research groups in North America began studying the plant as a potential treatment for diabetes, following a recommendation[span_21](start_span)[span_21](end_span).
  • Scientific Serendipity (1949): A technician named Halina Czajkowski Robinson discovered that, while the plant did not treat diabetes, it caused a significant reduction in white blood cells[span_22](start_span)[span_22](end_span).
  • Isolation of Active Compounds (1958): Scientist Charles Beers extracted alkaloid compounds with anti-cancer properties (Vinblastine and Vincristine)[span_23](start_span)[span_23](end_span).

3. Medical Information Overview

First: Medicinal Uses

  1. Anticancer Treatment: Plant-derived alkaloids (such as Vinblastine and Vincristine) are extracted and used as the basis for chemotherapy drugs for several types of cancer, most notably[span_24](start_span)[span_24](end_span):
    • Leukemia: Especially acute lymphoblastic leukemia in children[span_25](start_span)[span_25](end_span).
    • Lymphomas: Such as Hodgkin's and non-Hodgkin's lymphomas[span_26](start_span)[span_26](end_span).
    • Other Cancers: Solid tumors, breast cancer, and testicular cancer[span_27](start_span)[span_27](end_span).
  2. Traditional Antidiabetic Use: In traditional folk medicine across regions of Asia and Africa, periwinkle leaf infusions were historically used to help lower blood sugar levels in diabetic patients[span_28](start_span)[span_28](end_span). *(Medical note: Consuming it randomly as a tea is not recommended due to high toxicity)*[span_29](start_span)[span_29](end_span).
  3. Antioxidant and Antibacterial Properties: Laboratory studies have shown that the extracts contain compounds that combat certain types of bacteria and fungi and reduce inflammation, although the primary clinical application remains in precisely manufactured pharmaceutical derivatives[span_30](start_span)[span_30](end_span).

Second: Side Effects & Precautions

Due to the potency of its active constituents and high cellular toxicity, periwinkle is extremely dangerous if used haphazardly or via traditional folk methods[span_31](start_span)[span_31](end_span):

  1. High Toxicity: Consuming plant leaves directly or drinking them as an herbal tea is extremely dangerous and strictly prohibited, as it causes severe damage to the nervous system and body organs[span_32](start_span)[span_32](end_span).
  2. Side Effects of Derived Medications (Vinca Alkaloids):
    • Neurotoxicity: Such as tingling and numbness in the extremities and muscle weakness[span_33](start_span)[span_33](end_span).
    • Bone Marrow Suppression: Leading to a drop in white blood cells (increasing infection susceptibility) and low platelet counts[span_34](start_span)[span_34](end_span).
    • Temporary hair loss, nausea, and vomiting[span_35](start_span)[span_35](end_span).
  3. Contraindications:
    • Pregnancy and Breastfeeding: Strictly prohibited as it causes fetal malformations and severe harm[span_36](start_span)[span_36](end_span).
    • Individuals suffering from bone marrow depression or severe neurological disorders[span_37](start_span)[span_37](end_span).
