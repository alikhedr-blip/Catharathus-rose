INTRODUCTION

First documented by European botanists in the

mid-18th century (1750s) after seeds were brought

from Madagascar to Paris and London, the plant

received its first formal classification in 1759 when

Carl Linnaeus named it Vinca rosea. It retained this

name until 1837, when Scottish botanist George Don

recognized distinct floral structures and officially

reclassified it under the new genus Catharanthus.

01. WHEN

Catharanthus roseus is native exclusively to the

tropical island of Madagascar, off the southeastern

coast of Africa in the Indian Ocean. In its wild native

state, the plant evolved as an essential part of

Madagascar’s unique coastal ecosystem, thriving in

dry sandy dunes, limestone outcrops, forest edges,

and arid grassland biomes.Beginning in the

mid-18th century, European botanical expeditions

transported seeds and live specimens from

Madagascar to major botanical hubs in Europe,

such as the Jardin des Plantes in Paris and the

Chelsea Physic Garden in London�
