Project Medicinal Plant: Vinca Plant

Common Names: Periwinkle, pink periwinkle, rose periwinkle, Madagascar periwinkle[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span).

Latin (Botanic) Name: Catharanthus roseus (L.)[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span)

Family: Apocynaceae (Dogbane family)[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span)

History & Overview

The natural Vinca plant ("Flowering Vinca") is considered a major treasure in the fields of pharmacy and pharmacognosy[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span). The plant contains over 130 indole alkaloids[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span). The most medically significant of these—from which pharmaceutical agents are derived—include:

  1. Vincristine (VCR)
    * Structure: A dimeric indole alkaloid composed of two units: catharanthine and vindoline[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span).
    * Pharmaceutical Significance: Plays a big role in treating childhood leukemias[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span). Increased the survival rate for childhood leukemia from less than 10% in 1960 to 90% today[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span).
  2. Vinblastine (VLB)
    * Structure: Structurally very similar to vincristine, differing only in a chemical group (a methyl group instead of a formyl group on the vindoline ring); however, this slight difference results in distinct pharmacokinetic properties and activity profiles[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span).
    * Significance: Inhibits cancer cell division; used in the treatment of breast, lung, ovarian, and testicular cancers[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span).
  3. Semi-synthetic derivatives
    * Due to the low natural yield of these alkaloids from the plant, the pharmaceutical industry has developed semi-synthetic derivatives such as Vinorelbine and Vindesine to improve efficacy and reduce toxicity[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span).

Ethnobotanical History & Discovery

  • Native Origin (Madagascar): Malagasy people including fishermen chewed its leaves to suppress hunger and fatigue; it was also used in traditional medicine[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span).
  • Historical Spread: The plant was dispersed by seafarers across the Indian Ocean during the era of Swahili trade (800–1450 AD)[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span).
  • First European Record (1658): Étienne de Flacourt, governor of a French colony in Madagascar, documented the plant and sent its seeds to France[span_15](start_span)[span_15](end_span).
  • Chelsea Physic Garden: Philip Miller was the first to cultivate the plant and publish illustrations of it in Britain, which facilitated its spread throughout Europe[span_16](start_span)[span_16](end_span).
  • Modern Medical Discovery (1920s–1950s): Two research groups in North America began studying the plant as a potential treatment for diabetes following a recommendation[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span). In 1949, a technician named Halina Czajkowski Robinson discovered that while it didn't treat diabetes, it caused a significant reduction in white blood cells, leading to the isolation of anti-cancer alkaloids in 1958 by scientist Charles Beers[span_18](start_span)[span_18](end_span).

Medical Information Overview

First: Medicinal Uses

  1. Anticancer Treatment: Plant-derived alkaloids (Vinblastine and Vincristine) are extracted and used as the basis for chemotherapy drugs for several types of cancer, most notably:
    • Leukemia: Especially acute lymphoblastic leukemia in children[span_19](start_span)[span_19](end_span).
    • Lymphomas: Such as Hodgkin's and non-Hodgkin's lymphomas[span_20](start_span)[span_20](end_span).
    • Other solid tumors and cancers like breast and testicular cancer[span_21](start_span)[span_21](end_span).
  2. Traditional Antidiabetic Use: In traditional folk medicine across regions of Asia and Africa, periwinkle leaf infusions were historically used to help lower blood sugar levels in diabetic patients. *(Medical note: Consuming it randomly as a tea is not recommended due to high toxicity)*[span_22](start_span)[span_22](end_span).
  3. Antioxidant and Antibacterial Properties: Laboratory studies have shown that extracts contain compounds that combat certain types of bacteria and fungi and reduce inflammation, though clinical application remains in pharmaceutical derivatives[span_23](start_span)[span_23](end_span).

Second: Side Effects & Precautions

  1. High Toxicity: Consuming plant leaves directly or drinking them as an herbal tea is extremely dangerous and strictly prohibited, as it causes severe damage to the nervous system and body organs[span_24](start_span)[span_24](end_span).
  2. Side Effects of Derived Medications (Vinca Alkaloids):
    • Neurotoxicity: Tingling and numbness in the extremities and muscle weakness[span_25](start_span)[span_25](end_span).
    • Bone Marrow Suppression: Leading to a drop in white blood cells (increasing infection susceptibility) and low platelet counts[span_26](start_span)[span_26](end_span).
    • Temporary hair loss, nausea, and vomiting[span_27](start_span)[span_27](end_span).
  3. Contraindications:
    • Pregnancy and Breastfeeding: Strictly prohibited as it causes fetal malformations and severe harm[span_28](start_span)[span_28](end_span).
    • Individuals suffering from bone marrow depression or severe neurological disorders[span_29](start_span)[span_29](end_span).
